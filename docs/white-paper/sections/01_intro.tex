\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Chicago restricts short-term rental (STR) activity through ordinance-driven prohibitions aggregated to building addresses; after registration, policymakers block qualifying units so they cannot circulate through platforms such as Airbnb.
Whether those prohibitions materially change neighborhood rents motivates this brief: owners may repurpose dwellings toward long-term contracts, relieving pressure on rents, yet compositional shocks and macro trends can confound simple before--after contrasts.

\Citet{goodman-bacon2021difference} demonstrate that conventional two-way fixed effects with staggered treatment timing implicitly mix ``clean'' comparisons with contrasts among already-treated units, potentially overturning causal sign or magnitude whenever treatment effects evolve across cohorts.
We therefore present evidence in a deliberate ladder: (i)~a \emph{naive} TWFE event study on the trend-matched panel---what readers expect first, but heterogeneity-sensitive; (ii)~our \emph{preferred} full-panel Callaway--Sant'Anna average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) before any matching restriction; (iii)~robustness on the matched sample, alternative treatment definitions, and design-parameter sweeps; and (iv)~residualized Callaway--Sant'Anna estimates with ACS covariates and tract-specific linear trends, which improve pre-trend fit but live on a different scale than raw rent levels (see \texttt{docs/CS\_WITH\_CONTROLS.md}).

The analytic scope is narrowly administrative: prohibition dates come from disclosed city records coupled to spatial assignments of buildings to Census tracts, while rents combine Zillow's Observed Rent Index with a projected ZIP--tract crosswalk emitted by downstream processors.
Slides and appendix tables summarize adoption momentum, parallel-trends diagnostics, geography, and sensitivity grids documented in \texttt{docs/PREFERRED\_SPEC.md} and \texttt{docs/robustness/}; city partners may reproduce results via \texttt{docs/DID\_CS\_RUNBOOK.md}.
