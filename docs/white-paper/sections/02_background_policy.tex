\section{Policy background}\label{sec:policy}

The Shared Housing Ordinance requires registration and can ultimately prohibit entire buildings from operating as short-term rentals when the city records a qualifying STR ban.
Because enforcement depends on casework, inspection, and signed dates, prohibitions roll out slowly and unevenly across neighborhoods; some tracts never record a ban while others align with clusters of apartment towers or single-room-occupancy conversions.

For policy partners, the salient questions are whether prohibitions change neighborhood rent pressure and how quickly any effects appear.
Descriptive maps (\texttt{did\_spatial\_sample*.png}) illustrate where prohibitions concentrate relative to the trend-matched census tracts feeding the causal sample, while adoption curves (\texttt{did\_adoption\_curve.png}) measure administrative exposure over time.
Those visuals precede formal identification because they articulate who is exposed and when bans accelerate.
