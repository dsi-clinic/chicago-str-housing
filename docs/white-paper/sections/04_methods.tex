\section{Empirical Strategy}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{The Problem with Two-Way Fixed Effects}

The standard approach to estimating treatment effects in staggered adoption settings is a two-way fixed effects (TWFE) regression of the form

\[
Y_{it} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \beta D_{it} + \varepsilon_{it},
\]

where $\alpha_i$ and $\lambda_t$ are unit and time fixed effects, $D_{it}$ is the treatment indicator, and $\beta$ is interpreted as the average treatment effect. In settings with a single treatment date and homogeneous effects, this estimator is unbiased. Under staggered adoption with treatment effect heterogeneity, however, TWFE can produce severely misleading estimates.

\citet{goodman-bacon2021difference} shows that the TWFE coefficient decomposes into a weighted average of all pairwise two-by-two comparisons between groups treated at different times. Crucially, some of these comparisons use already-treated units as controls for later-treated units, assigning implicit weights that can be negative when treatment effects vary across cohorts or over time. In our setting --- where early-adopting tracts concentrated in high-demand neighborhoods may respond differently to prohibitions than late-adopting tracts spreading cityward --- this contamination risk is substantial. \citet{de-chaisemartin2020two} derive analogous concerns, showing that any estimator relying on a two-way fixed effects structure can produce estimates of the wrong sign when effects are heterogeneous.

We implement TWFE alongside our preferred estimator for diagnostic comparison but do not use it as a primary causal claim. The contrast between TWFE and the Callaway--Sant'Anna estimates in our results illustrates empirically how large the heterogeneity bias can be in this setting (Section~\ref{sec:results-main-2feat}).

\subsection{Callaway--Sant'Anna Group-Time ATT}

Our preferred estimator is the Callaway--Sant'Anna (CS) group-time average treatment effect on the treated \citep{callaway2021difference}. The estimator proceeds by defining treatment cohorts --- groups of units sharing a common first-treatment period --- and estimating cohort-specific, period-specific ATTs.

Formally, let $G_i$ denote the first calendar period in which unit $i$ is treated ($G_i = \infty$ for never-treated units), and let $C$ denote a comparison group of units not yet treated by period $t$. For cohort $g$ and period $t$, the group-time ATT is

\[
\mathrm{ATT}(g, t) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_t(g) - Y_t(0) \mid G_i = g\bigr],
\]

which is identified under the parallel trends assumption as

\[
\mathrm{ATT}(g, t) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid G_i = g\bigr] - \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid i \in C\bigr].
\]

Each cohort-time cell compares the change in rents from the period before treatment ($g-1$) to period $t$ for treated tracts, against the same change for comparison units. Because already-treated units are never used as controls, the estimator avoids the negative-weighting problem of TWFE.

\paragraph{Comparison group.} We use never-treated tracts (those with $G_i = \infty$) as the primary comparison group. This is a stricter and more transparent choice than using not-yet-treated units as controls, which introduces additional assumptions about the timing of treatment effects for later-adopting cohorts. Sensitivity to this choice is discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness-overview}.

\paragraph{Aggregation.} We aggregate group-time ATTs to the event-time dimension by computing
\[
\overline{\mathrm{ATT}}(\ell) = \sum_{g} w_g \cdot \mathrm{ATT}(g, g + \ell),
\]
where $\ell$ denotes the number of periods since first treatment, and weights $w_g$ are proportional to cohort size. This produces event-study plots tracing the dynamic treatment path and a pooled post-period ATT that averages across all positive event times. The doubly-robust (DR) variant of the estimator, following \citet{santanna2020doubly}, also incorporates observed covariates in the outcome model to improve efficiency.

\subsection{Parallel Trends Assumption and Pre-Trend Evidence}\label{sec:methods-parallel-trends}

The CS estimator requires the conditional parallel trends assumption:

\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_t(0) - Y_{g-1}(0) \mid G_i = g\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_t(0) - Y_{g-1}(0) \mid i \in C\bigr] \quad \text{for all } t < g.
\]

In words: absent the prohibition, treated tracts would have followed the same rent trend as never-treated tracts in every pre-treatment period.

This assumption is violated in our setting. The full-panel CS event study (Section~\ref{sec:results-pretrend}) reveals a V-shaped pre-treatment divergence: the group-time ATT at $t = -8$ is approximately $-$\$16/month, dipping significantly below zero before recovering toward zero at $t = -1$. A joint Wald test on all pre-period group-time ATTs rejects the null of flat pre-trends at $p < 0.001$. This pattern is economically coherent --- STR prohibitions were adopted disproportionately in tracts that were already on faster rent-growth trajectories --- but it means the standard parallel trends assumption does not hold.

We address this in two ways. First, we document the violation explicitly and interpret raw ATTs as an upper bound on the plausible effect size, noting that the positive post-period estimates may partly reflect continuation of the pre-ban divergence rather than a prohibition-caused rent change. Second, we apply the sensitivity framework of \citet{rbs2023} to bound the degree of violation. The HonestDiD smoothness constraint $M$ captures the maximum allowable month-to-month change in the counterfactual trend relative to the observed pre-period. Using the largest observed pre-period change ($M = 2.71$ \$/month per period, derived from the second differences of the pre-period TWFE coefficients), the identified set for the average ATT remains consistent with positive effects throughout the post-period window. This does not restore point identification but provides a credible lower bound: effects are positive even under the most pessimistic violation permitted by $M = 2.71$.

Pre-trend tests themselves are underpowered in this design. \citet{roth2022pretest} documents that standard parallel-trends pre-tests have low power against economically relevant violations, and that passing a pre-test provides little assurance of identification. The TWFE parallel-trends regression on our matched panel fails to reject at $p \approx 0.34$ despite economically large pre-period gaps ($\$7$--$\$16$/month). This result should not be read as evidence that parallel trends holds; it reflects the low power of the test relative to the observed violation magnitude.

\subsection{Pre-Treatment Trend Matching}\label{sec:methods-matching}

To strengthen the parallel trends assumption by comparing more similar units, we apply $k$-nearest-neighbor matching on pre-treatment rent trajectories. The \textbf{baseline} specification matches on two standardized features used in the published DSI clinic paper:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pre-treatment rent slope} (\texttt{pre\_trend\_slope})
  \item \textbf{Average pre-treatment rent} (\texttt{avg\_pre\_rent})
\end{enumerate}
Our \textbf{strict robustness} specification adds three lagged rent levels (\texttt{rent\_lag\_1}, \texttt{rent\_lag\_6}, \texttt{rent\_lag\_12}) relative to the panel-wide first treatment month to capture short-run trajectory shape.

All features are standardized before computing Euclidean distances. The baseline two-feature rule reproduces the clinic's headline matched ATT; the five-feature rule is more conservative and reveals V-shaped pre-period composition that slope-only matching can miss (Section~\ref{sec:results-5feat}).

The matched panel under five-feature matching retains 274 treated tracts and 176 never-treated controls (450 total tracts, out of 842 rent-panel tracts). Post-match standardized mean differences improve on slope (from 1.36 to 0.75) though material imbalance remains. The preferred matching depth is $k = 3$ (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness-k}).

\subsection{Estimator Ladder}

We organize our results around a deliberate sequence of increasingly restrictive specifications:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Full-panel CS (primary diagnostic).} Callaway--Sant'Anna on all rent-panel tracts with never-treated controls. This is the first result readers should inspect (the event study reveals the V-shape).

  \item \textbf{Two-feature matched CS (baseline headline).} CS on the matched panel using slope + level matching --- comparable to the published clinic specification (Section~\ref{sec:results-main-2feat}).

  \item \textbf{Five-feature matched CS (strict robustness).} Same estimator on a stricter matched panel using lagged rent levels (Section~\ref{sec:results-5feat}).

  \item \textbf{Naive TWFE (benchmark).} Conventional two-way fixed effects on the matched panel for comparison.

  \item \textbf{Residualized CS (appendix sensitivity).} Doubly-robust CS on rents residualized per equation~\eqref{eq:residualization}; magnitudes are not comparable to raw rent ATTs (Appendix~\ref{app:residualized}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Inference}

Standard errors are computed analytically by the \texttt{csdid} implementation following \citet{callaway2021difference}. Cluster-robust inference clusters at the census tract level to account for within-tract serial correlation in monthly rent observations. All event-study figures display 95\% pointwise confidence intervals; pre-period joint tests use the Wald statistic against the null that all pre-period group-time ATTs equal zero simultaneously.
