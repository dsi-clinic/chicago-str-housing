\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Pre-Treatment Dynamics: The V-Shape}\label{sec:results-pretrend}

Figure~\ref{fig:cs-full-panel} is the first result readers should inspect. The full-panel Callaway--Sant'Anna event study --- using all rent-panel tracts and never-treated units as the comparison group --- reveals a pronounced V-shaped departure from parallel trends in the pre-treatment period. Group-time ATTs fall steadily from approximately $-\$8$/month at $t = -12$ to a minimum near $-\$16$/month at $t = -8$, then recover monotonically back toward zero at $t = -1$. A joint Wald test on all pre-period group-time ATTs rejects the flat pre-trend null at $p < 0.001$; all 11 pre-period coefficients are individually statistically significant.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.93\linewidth]{../../output/did-cs-whitepaper-2feat-threshold/did_callaway_santanna_event_study_full_panel.png}
    \caption{Full-panel Callaway--Sant'Anna event study (preferred sample before matching). Pre-period coefficients depart systematically from zero in a V-shaped pattern; post-period dynamics must be interpreted in light of this violation.}
    \label{fig:cs-full-panel}
\end{figure}

The V-shaped pre-period has a clear economic interpretation. STR prohibition activity was concentrated in tracts experiencing faster pre-ban rent growth. The dip at $t = -8$ followed by recovery toward zero at $t = -1$ is consistent with a temporary divergence: treated tracts were on a steeper appreciation trajectory than never-treated tracts. This selection into treatment is economically coherent but means the standard parallel trends assumption does not hold.

Critically, the TWFE parallel trends regression on the matched panel fails to reject at $p \approx 0.34$, despite economically large pre-period gaps. This discrepancy illustrates the low power of standard pre-trend tests documented by \citet{roth2022pretest}. We therefore treat parallel trends as a core identification challenge and report HonestDiD bounds rather than claiming the assumption is satisfied (Appendix~\ref{app:parallel-trends}).

\subsection{Main Result: Two-Feature Matching Baseline}\label{sec:results-main-2feat}

Our \textbf{baseline matched specification} follows the published DSI clinic design: each ever-treated tract is matched to $k=3$ never-treated tracts on two standardized pre-treatment features --- rent slope and average pre-rent --- before applying Callaway--Sant'Anna on the matched panel. This two-feature rule reproduces the clinic's headline event-study shape and point estimates on the same data (Appendix~\ref{app:boss-reconciliation}).

    \label{tab:main-att-2feat}
    \input{../../output/did-cs-whitepaper-2feat-threshold/tables/tab_main_att.tex}

Under two-feature matching, the matched-sample CS ATT is \$4.60/month (Table~\ref{tab:main-att-2feat}). The full-panel CS ATT is \$20.65/month and is identical by construction across matching specifications; given the V-shaped pre-period, it likely mixes any genuine prohibition effect with continuation of pre-ban divergence. The matched-sample estimate restricts to tracts with comparable pre-treatment slope and level. Relative to the published clinic matched ATT (about \$6/month on an earlier binary-treatment specification), our threshold-panel estimate is in the same positive range but somewhat smaller, consistent with the intensity rule delaying treatment timing for some tracts.

\subsection{Five-Feature Matching: Stricter Trajectory Control}\label{sec:results-5feat}

Two-feature matching aligns treated and control tracts on direction and average level but can miss differences in \emph{trajectory shape}. We therefore report a stricter five-feature specification that adds lagged rent levels at 1, 6, and 12 months before the panel-wide first treatment month (Section~\ref{sec:methods-matching}). This specification is our preferred \textbf{robustness layer} for balance on short-run curvature, not a replacement for the baseline clinic comparability result.

Table~\ref{tab:matching-att-comparison} compares pooled ATTs across the two matching rules. Under five-feature matching, the pre-period V-shape in the event study is often more visible, indicating that some slope-only matches were masking short-run curvature differences. We interpret the two-feature estimate as the clinic-comparable headline and the five-feature estimate as a robustness check on match definition (Appendix~\ref{app:matching}).

\subsection{Treatment Intensity vs.\ Binary Definition}\label{sec:results-binary}

Our preferred treatment indicator (intensity threshold, $p = 0.25$) classifies a tract as treated only when prohibition intensity exceeds a year-specific share cutoff. \subsection{Residualized Specifications}\label{sec:results-residualized}

As a sensitivity check on functional-form assumptions, we residualize rents on ACS covariates and tract-specific linear time trends estimated on pre-treatment months only, then re-estimate Callaway--Sant'Anna on the preconditioned outcome:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualization}
    \widetilde{Y}_{it} = Y_{it} - \widehat{X}_i'\hat{\beta} - \hat{\alpha}_i - \hat{\delta}_i \cdot t,
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{X}_i'\hat{\beta}$ removes tract-level ACS covariates (standardized), and $\hat{\alpha}_i + \hat{\delta}_i \cdot t$ removes a tract-specific linear trend fit using pre-treatment observations only; the outcome is re-centered at the original sample mean so that levels remain interpretable on the preconditioned scale.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.93\linewidth]{../../output/did-cs-whitepaper-2feat-threshold/did_callaway_santanna_event_study_with_controls.png}
    \caption{Dynamic CS on ACS- and linear-detrend-residualized outcomes under two-feature matching (sensitivity only; magnitudes not comparable to raw rent ATTs).}
    \label{fig:cs-controls-main}
\end{figure}

The residualized pooled ATT can differ sharply in sign from raw rent ATTs when linear detrending extrapolates the late pre-period recovery slope forward (Appendix~\ref{app:residualized}). Appendix~\ref{app:matching-residualized} reports how the residualized ATT shifts under two-feature vs.\ five-feature matching.

\subsection{Spillover Analysis and SUTVA}\label{sec:results-spillovers}

A core DiD assumption is SUTVA: treatment in one tract does not affect outcomes elsewhere. We test spatial spillovers using a queen-contiguity design that re-labels never-treated tracts adjacent to any treated tract as treated from the earliest neighbor prohibition month, comparing them to never-treated tracts with no treated neighbor (pure controls). Original prohibited tracts are excluded from this second-stage panel.

The spillover design yields a pooled ATT of \$25.8/month (Table~\ref{tab:spillover-summary}), indicating that adjacent never-treated tracts experience rent increases when neighbors adopt prohibitions. Pure controls are sparse ($n=34$), so this estimate should be read as supportive evidence of positive spatial spillovers rather than a precisely calibrated headline.
